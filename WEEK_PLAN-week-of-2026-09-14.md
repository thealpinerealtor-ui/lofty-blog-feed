## Monday, September 14 — Local market update
**Working title:** Ask Versus Close: What Flathead Sellers Are Actually Getting
**Angle:** Every seller watches list prices; almost none watch the spread between what a house
asks and what it closes at. Pull the current Flathead County / Montana Regional MLS figures and
work the list-to-sale ratio: what the average discount off final asking price looks like right
now, how that differs from the discount off *original* asking price once reductions are counted,
and why those two numbers being far apart is the clearest sign a market is negotiating rather
than stalling. Cody May / Leo Pareja treatment — the figure first, then what a seller does with
it on Tuesday morning.

## Tuesday, September 15 — Divorce home sales
**Working title:** Showings While You're Both Still Living There
**Angle:** The month between listing and closing is the part nobody plans for when neither spouse
has moved out yet. Practical and process-only: how a showing schedule gets built when two people
need notice and neither wants to coordinate directly, why a single written calendar beats text
threads, what to do about children's schedules and the school run, keeping personal papers and
valuables secured while strangers walk through, whose job it is to have the house presentable on
a given day, how a lockbox and confirmed-appointments-only policy lowers the temperature, and
what happens when one party starts declining showings. Empathetic, no side-taking, no judgment.
"Your attorney drives what the decree requires — this is the logistics side only."

## Wednesday, September 16 — Buyer guides
**Working title:** What You Sign Before You Tour: Buyer Agency in Montana
**Angle:** Buyers now sign a written agreement before they see the inside of a house, and most
sign it at the door without reading a line of it. Plain-English walk-through of what that document
actually sets: the term length and how to keep it short at first, whether it's exclusive and what
that means if you find a house on your own, the scope — one property, one town, or the whole
valley — how the agent's compensation is stated and who can end up paying it, what the protection
period does after the agreement ends, and how to cancel one that isn't working. Frame it as a
buyer's tool rather than a hurdle: a written scope of work is how you hold someone to a standard.
Process only — "your broker's forms and your attorney govern the specifics."

## Thursday, September 17 — Estate & probate sales
**Working title:** The Empty House in Winter: Protecting an Inherited Home Until It Sells
**Angle:** Probate runs longer than the season does, and a Flathead house sitting empty through
its first unattended winter is where an estate quietly loses real money. What the family needs
handled before the first hard freeze: telling the insurer the home is vacant and why an ordinary
policy may not cover it once nobody lives there, heat left on and at what setting versus draining
the system, water shut off at the main, pipes on exterior walls, the water heater, a snow-removal
arrangement so the drive stays passable for showings and so the house doesn't look empty, roof and
ice damming, keeping mail collected, and a set of eyes on the property on a schedule. Compassionate
and concrete — this is the practical care of an asset the family is holding on someone else's
behalf. Disclaimer: confirm coverage terms with the insurer and estate authority with the attorney.

## Friday, September 18 — Community & lifestyle
**Working title:** Larch Season: The Two Weeks the Valley Turns Gold
**Angle:** Western larch is a conifer that drops its needles, and for a short window in late
September and October the drainages around the Flathead go yellow-gold in a way that surprises
people who moved here for the summers. Where it happens and when it typically peaks, the drives
and trails that show it best, how the timing shifts with elevation, what the weather usually does
in that stretch, and why this is the season a lot of buyers decide the valley is worth the winter.
Amenities, roads, and places only — no characterization of who lives where. Written as a reason to
be outside, not as a pitch.

## Saturday, September 19 — Seller guides
**Working title:** Reading an Offer: What Sellers Look At After the Price
**Angle:** The highest number on the page loses to a cleaner offer more often than sellers expect,
and by the time that becomes obvious the decision has usually already been made. What actually
determines whether an offer closes: financing type and what a preapproval letter does and doesn't
promise, earnest money as a measure of commitment, the length and breadth of the inspection window,
appraisal-gap language and what it's worth when values are soft, the closing date measured against
the seller's own next move, requests for a rent-back, who's asking for what in closing costs, and
whether the buyer's own house has to sell first. How to compare two offers side by side on terms
rather than headline price. Complements Monday's ask-versus-close math from the seller's chair.

## Sunday, September 20 — Brand / authority
**Working title:** When the Right Advice Is "Don't"
**Angle:** The pieces this month have been about planning and sequence; this one is about the
harder part of the job, which is telling a client the deal in front of them isn't the one to do.
Anonymized and hypothetical: the house that fits everything on the list except the thing the
client will care about in three years, the offer that should be countered instead of accepted, the
purchase that a client can afford on paper and shouldn't stretch for. Why an agent whose income
depends on a transaction closing has to be willing to argue against one, what it costs in the short
run, and why the clients who got told no are the ones who send people. No agency named, no war
stories, no hype — the standard, plainly stated.
