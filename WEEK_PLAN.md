## Monday, August 24 — Local market update
**Working title:** What a Rate Drop Is Actually Worth in the Flathead
**Angle:** Mortgage rates have now fallen two weeks running — 6.65% on the 30-year as of August 20, down from 6.67%, per Freddie Mac's PMMS — while Flathead sellers are still cutting prices at a heavy clip. Rather than recite inventory again, this one does the payment math: what roughly a tenth of a point is actually worth per month on a median Flathead home, why that number is smaller than buyers hope and larger than sellers assume, and what it takes for cheaper money to break a standoff market. Cody May / Leo Pareja treatment: data → what it means for you.

## Tuesday, August 25 — Divorce home sales
**Working title:** Dividing the Equity: What the House Is Actually Worth in a Divorce
**Angle:** Most couples arguing about the house are arguing about the wrong number. There are three different values in play — what an appraiser says it's worth, what the market will actually pay, and what's left after payoff, commissions, taxes, and repairs — and only the third one gets split. This walks through where the money goes between contract and closing, why an appraisal and a listing price are answering different questions, and why getting a real net sheet early ends more arguments than it starts. Empathetic, process-focused, zero judgment, no legal or tax advice — "confirm the split with your attorney."

## Wednesday, August 26 — Buyer guides
**Working title:** Buying Land in the Flathead: What to Check Before the View
**Angle:** Plenty of people move here intending to build, and land is the one purchase where the listing photos tell you almost nothing. What to verify before you're emotionally committed: legal and physical access (recorded easement vs. "the neighbor's always let us drive through"), zoning and county setbacks, whether power and fiber actually reach the property line and what a drop costs if they don't, water rights and well feasibility, septic suitability and soils, floodplain and wetland mapping, and how to structure a due-diligence window long enough to answer all of it. Checklist-driven, plain English, distinct from the well-and-septic piece by staying on the raw-land side of the fence.

## Thursday, August 27 — Estate & probate sales
**Working title:** When the Heirs Don't Agree: Selling a Family Home Together
**Angle:** The hardest estate sales aren't complicated legally — they're complicated because three siblings want three different things, and one of them lives in the house. What the personal representative can and can't decide alone, why an independent valuation defuses more conflict than any conversation does, how to handle the heir who wants to buy the others out, what happens when someone simply won't sign, and why setting a written timeline early keeps a family from litigating over a roof. Compassionate, never taking sides, with "consult your attorney" language throughout.

## Friday, August 28 — Community & lifestyle
**Working title:** September in the Flathead: The Valley's Quietest Month
**Angle:** The week after Labor Day, the valley changes character — traffic on 93 thins out, Going-to-the-Sun and the Glacier trailheads empty, the larch start turning gold up the drainages in late September, the lake stays warm for weeks after the crowds go home, and restaurant tables in Whitefish and Bigfork open back up. What's actually open versus what closes for shoulder season, what the weather typically does, and why September is the month locals quietly guard. Amenities and places only; no characterizations of who lives where.

## Saturday, August 29 — Seller guides
**Working title:** Pricing Into a Slow Market Without Getting Chased Down
**Angle:** When roughly one in three active listings has already cut its price, the pricing conversation stops being about optimism and starts being about sequence. Why the first two weeks of showings are the only honest feedback you'll get, what an "aspirational" number actually costs in days on market and in the final sale price, the difference between a reduction that resets a listing and one that just follows the market down, and how to set a number you can hold. Complements Monday's rate math from the seller's side of the table.
