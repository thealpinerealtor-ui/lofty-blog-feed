## Tuesday, September 29 — Divorce home sales
**Working title:** One Agent or Two: How Divorcing Couples Actually List the House in Montana
**Angle:** A question that comes up before the sign goes in the yard — does the couple hire one
neutral agent to represent the sale, or does each spouse bring their own? Walk through both paths:
a single dual/limited-representation listing agent (Montana's disclosure and written-consent
requirements, how it centralizes communication and keeps one version of the truth), versus
separate agents on each side (when that actually lowers conflict instead of raising it — high
distrust, contested price opinions, one spouse not speaking to the other). Neutral on which is
"better" — it depends on the couple. Empathetic, process-only, no side-taking. "Your attorney
weighs in on anything the decree already addresses."

## Wednesday, September 30 — Buyer guides
**Working title:** New Construction vs. Resale in the Flathead: What the Timeline Actually Looks Like
**Angle:** Buyers comparing a new-build lot to a resale house are usually comparing price, not time
— and time is where new construction surprises people. Permitting and builder-queue realities in
this market, how far out a rate lock reaches versus a typical build length, what change orders do
to both the budget and the calendar, HOA/covenant review on new developments versus established
neighborhoods, and how a resale's 30-45 day close compares on paper. Not anti-new-construction —
just an honest timeline comparison so a buyer picks with eyes open.

## Thursday, October 1 — Estate & probate sales
**Working title:** How Long Montana Probate Actually Takes Before a House Can List
**Angle:** Heirs often assume a home can list the week after a death; in practice, listing depends
on when a personal representative actually holds authority to sign. Walk the realistic phases:
informal vs. formal probate in Montana, PR appointment timing, the creditor-claim window, what
authority looks like on paper (Letters Testamentary/of Administration) before a listing agreement
is enforceable, and the common things that add weeks — missing wills, out-of-state heirs needing
to sign remotely, disagreement among heirs. Sets expectations early so the family isn't guessing.
Compassionate, concrete, disclaimer: "consult the attorney handling the estate for anything
specific to this probate."

## Saturday, October 3 — Seller guides
**Working title:** Listing Photos in October Light: What Changes About Marketing a Home as the Days Get Short
**Angle:** As daylight shortens into fall, the window for photographing a home well gets narrower
and most sellers don't adjust their showing/photo schedule for it. What actually changes: the
best remaining light hours for exterior shots, why a listing photographed in flat afternoon light
underperforms online, twilight and interior-light options once the sun sets before 7, and timing
a photo shoot before a home sits through a full gray stretch. Practical, not gimmicky — the goal
is more clicks in the first 48 hours a listing is live, which is when most of its traffic happens.

## Sunday, October 4 — Brand / authority
**Working title:** Everyone Reads From the Same Page: The Habit That Prevents Most Deal Blowups
**Angle:** Most deals that fall apart late didn't fail on the numbers — they failed because two
parties were working from two different understandings of where things stood. The habit borrowed
from briefings in a higher-stakes line of work: one update, to everyone who needs it, at the same
time, instead of five different conversations that all drift. What that looks like applied to a
transaction — buyer, seller, both agents, lender, title, inspector all seeing the same status at
the same moments — and why it prevents more blowups than any negotiating tactic does. No agency
named, no war stories, the standard plainly stated.
