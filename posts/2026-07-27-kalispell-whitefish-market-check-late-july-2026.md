Late July is when the Flathead Valley housing market hits its widest point of the year. More homes on the market than any other season, more buyers in town, more showings on the calendar — and, every year, more sellers and buyers misreading what that peak actually means.

So let's do what we do every Monday: numbers first, then the read.

## The Numbers Right Now

**Kalispell** (source: Houzeo market data, June 2026):

- **Median home price: $590,200** — up 5.4% year over year
- **Homes for sale: 481**
- **Median days on market: 58** — down about 20% from a year ago
- **Sale-to-list ratio: 97.98%** — sellers are getting roughly 98 cents on the asking dollar
- **Months of supply: 3.1** — the textbook definition of a balanced market

**Whitefish** (source: Zillow, May–June 2026): the typical home value sits at about **$873,000**, essentially flat — down 0.3% over the past year — with roughly **251 active listings**. Median sale price came in just over **$1,000,000** in April 2026, while the median *list* price in May was about $1,179,500. Notice that gap between what sellers are asking and what homes actually close at. We'll come back to it.

On the financing side: the **30-year fixed averaged 6.58%** for the week of July 23, 2026, per Freddie Mac — up slightly from 6.55% the week before. Rates have been grinding sideways in the mid-6s for months now.

## What the Summer Peak Actually Means

Here's the question most people never stop to ask: *if this is the busiest the market gets all year, why do so many summer listings sit?*

Because peak inventory cuts both ways. Yes, more buyers are shopping in July and August than in January. But your home is also competing against more listings than at any other point on the calendar — 481 of them in Kalispell alone. The summer peak doesn't reward *listing*. It rewards *standing out*.

### If You're Thinking About an August Listing

Three things the June data tells you:

**The market is moving faster than last year.** Fifty-eight median days on market, down 20% year over year, means correctly priced homes are finding buyers at a healthy clip. That's the good news.

**The market is also unforgiving about price.** A 98% sale-to-list ratio sounds strong until you understand what it hides: that average is built from homes that priced right and sold near ask — and homes that overshot, sat, and cut. Look at Whitefish, where the median list price ran roughly $175,000 above the median sale price this spring. That gap is the sound of aspirational pricing meeting a disciplined market.

**The clock matters more in August than in May.** List in early August and you're in front of peak buyer traffic. Miss your pricing and spend 60 days chasing the market down, and you're relisting into October — when the tourists have gone home and the serious-buyer pool thins out. In my old line of work we planned the mission backward from the objective. Same principle here: if you want to be closed before the snow flies, the prep work starts now, not Labor Day.

### If You're Making an August Offer

Flip everything above around and it's your playbook.

With 3.1 months of supply, you're in the most balanced conditions this valley has seen in years — neither side holds all the cards. That means the listings that have been sitting 60, 90, 120 days are where your leverage lives. The seller who priced to 2022 and has been watching showings dry up all summer is far more open to a conversation in August than they were in June.

And at 6.58%, financing strategy is worth as much as price negotiation. On a $590,000 Kalispell purchase, a seller-funded rate buydown can move your monthly payment more than a modest price cut — and sellers staring down a stale listing will often fund one. If you're a veteran or active-duty, your VA loan stacks on top of all of this: zero down, no PMI, competitive rates. As an Army veteran, walking fellow veterans through that benefit is one of the most satisfying parts of this job — it consistently buys more house than people expect.

### If Your Sale Isn't a "Normal" Sale

A percentage of every summer's transactions happen because life forced the timing — a divorce, an estate to settle, a court deadline that doesn't care about market seasonality. If that's your situation, the numbers above still apply, but the process needs a different kind of handling: tighter timelines, more parties signing off, and discretion throughout. That's a lane I work in deliberately, with a process built for exactly those conditions.

## The Bottom Line

The Flathead Valley in late July 2026 is balanced, brisk for well-priced homes, and brutal on wishful pricing. The summer peak gives you the most buyers — and the most competition — you'll see all year. It rewards the prepared on both sides of the table.
