It's Labor Day. By tomorrow morning the valley goes back to work, the summer lookers are gone, and what's left on the market is what's actually for sale.

So here's the question worth asking before you do anything else with your listing this week: when your house doesn't sell, how do you know whether that's the market or your price?

Most sellers never separate those two things. They read a headline about inventory or rates, decide the whole valley is slow, and settle in to wait. Sometimes that's right. Often it isn't — and the difference is expensive.

Numbers first, then the read.

## The Numbers: The Month That Just Ended

**Kalispell**, for the period August 6 through September 6, 2026 (source: Montana Regional MLS data via IDEAL Real Estate's Kalispell market report, that period):

- Active inventory: 504 properties, down 2.33% from the prior month
- New listings: 96, down 8.57%
- Average days on market, active listings: **116.67 days, up 12.18%**
- Price changes: **160 listings adjusted price, up 68.42%**
- Average list price, active inventory: $982,000, up 4.92%
- Asking price per square foot on new listings: $419, up 19.42%

**Whitefish**, same period, same source: 300 active properties, down 4.15%. New listings 52, down 11.86%. Average days on market **152, up 10.96%**. Price changes 46, *down* 22.03%. Average asking price about $2.5 million.

**Rates.** The Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed at 6.71% for the week of September 3, 2026 — up from 6.66% the week before, with the 15-year at 6.04%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.50% (source: Freddie Mac PMMS, September 3, 2026).

One note on the regional monthly figures: the Northwest Montana Association of REALTORS® county-level report still stops at July 2026. The August numbers land mid-September. So the freshest picture available today is the MLS activity above.

## Read the Average Again

116.67 days is the average for *active* Kalispell listings. Not sold. Active. Still sitting.

That is the number most sellers skip past, and it reframes everything. When the average unsold home in Kalispell has been on the market nearly four months, the 90-day listing isn't the sad outlier at the end of the row. **The 90-day listing is the market.** Whitefish is further out still, at 152 days.

Which means the comparison a seller actually needs isn't their house against "the valley." It's their house against two very different groups sharing the same MLS.

## The Two Cohorts Nobody Separates

**The aged group** has been out there since roughly May or June. They've had the showings. They've had the feedback. And 160 of them adjusted price in the last thirty days — against 504 active listings, that's close to one in three cutting inside a single month, at a pace 68% higher than the month before. That group is no longer guessing what the market will pay. It's finding out, in public, one reduction at a time.

**The new group** is the 96 listings that came on in the last month — and they came on asking 19.4% more per square foot than the prior month's new listings did.

Sit with that for a second. The homes that have been tested are cutting. The homes that haven't been tested are entering higher. Every new seller in Kalispell right now is pricing against the summer they hoped for, while their competition down the street is pricing against the summer that actually happened.

That gap closes exactly one way, and it isn't the aged listings coming back up.

## Whitefish Is Playing a Different Hand

Whitefish sellers sat longer — 152 days on average — and adjusted *less*, with price changes down 22%. Higher-end and second-home sellers usually have the balance sheet to wait, and many are choosing to. That's a legitimate strategy when there's no clock on you.

It is not a strategy that transfers. If you have a payment, a move, a job start, or a decree with a date on it, "wait it out" is borrowing from a future you haven't budgeted for. Carrying a Kalispell house through a Montana winter has a real monthly cost, and it doesn't pause because the listing does.

## What This Means Before Rates Come Up

They won't rescue you. 6.71%, five basis points higher than last week and twenty-one basis points above last September. Rates have been flat-to-slightly-up all summer, and if they do fall, they fall for every buyer and every waiting seller on the same morning — which puts you in a bigger crowd, not a better position.

The price you negotiate in a quiet October is permanent. The rate is the part that can be refinanced. Sellers have that backwards more often than not.

## The Bottom Line

If your home has been listed 90-plus days in the Flathead, you're in the largest cohort on the board, not a failing minority — and that's genuinely useful information, because it tells you the answer isn't panic.

But it also removes an excuse. When the average active listing is at 116 days and a third of them cut price last month, "the market is slow" stops explaining why the house three streets over went under contract in three weeks and yours didn't.

So the honest question for this week: do you actually know how your list price compares to what *closed* near you in the last 60 days — not what's asking, what closed? And if you found out today that you were 6% high, would you rather learn it now, in September, or in February after two more reductions?
