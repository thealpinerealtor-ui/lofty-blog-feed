Every year, right around the first week of August, the Flathead Valley market makes a quiet pivot. Nobody rings a bell. The lake is still busy, Glacier traffic is still backed up at West Glacier, and the listing photos still look like postcards. But underneath, the numbers start moving in a different direction — and if you're listing a home or writing an offer this month, you want to see the turn before everyone else does.

It's Monday, so let's do numbers first, then the read.

## The Numbers: Early August 2026

Here's what the most recent data shows for Kalispell and the surrounding market, covering roughly July 2 through August 2, 2026 (source: Montana Regional MLS data via IDEAL Real Estate's Kalispell market report, July 2026 period):

New listings came in at just 97 for the period — down 36.6% from June. That's the sharpest one-month pullback in fresh inventory we've seen all season.

Active listings sat at 525, down 3.67% from June. Total inventory has crested. The summer stack of homes is no longer growing — it's starting to thin as listings expire, withdraw, or go under contract without being replaced.

Average days on market for active listings stretched to 106, up more than 10% from June. The homes still sitting are sitting longer.

Average asking price per square foot slipped to $367, down about 1.8% — small, but it's the direction that matters. Sellers are starting to reach down toward the market instead of over it.

For context on closed sales: Kalispell's median sale price was $627,495 in June 2026, with sold properties averaging around 58 days on market (source: Movoto market trends, June 2026 data). Notice the gap — homes that actually sell move in about two months. The active inventory averaging 106 days? That's the overpriced tail.

And rates: the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed at 6.66% as of July 30, 2026, up from 6.58% the week before, with the 15-year at 6.04% (source: Freddie Mac PMMS, July 30, 2026). That's the highest reading in nearly a year.

## What the Turn Actually Is

Put those numbers together and you get the August pattern I see every year in this valley:

Sellers stop entering the market. The 36% drop in new listings isn't weakness — it's the calendar. Families who wanted to move before school starts have already listed. Anyone thinking "maybe next spring" has decided to wait. The window for fresh competition is closing.

Buyer traffic thins, but it changes character. The July crowds — half of them tourists dreaming through open houses between huckleberry stops — head home. Who's left in August and September? People who need to buy. Relocations, job starts, 1031 deadlines, life changes. Fewer showings, better showings.

The overpriced tail gets exposed. When homes are selling in 58 days and yours has been active for 100+, the market has already voted. In July, a seller could tell themselves the right buyer just hadn't come through yet. In August, that story stops holding.

## If You're Selling This Month

Two very different situations, two very different plays.

If you're already on the market and past 60 days: you're now chasing the market, and the worst version of that is the slow drip — $10,000 off every three weeks, trailing buyer expectations the whole way down. If you're going to adjust, adjust once, decisively, to a number that's inside the market instead of just closer to it. One meaningful correction in early August beats three timid ones through October.

If you're about to list: the turn is actually your friend. You're entering a market where new competition just dropped by a third and the remaining buyers are serious. A home that's priced against June's closed sales — not against your neighbor's hopeful asking price — can stand out sharply right now precisely because so much of the active inventory is stale. Preparation still matters more than timing: inspection-ready condition and honest pricing beat any calendar trick.

## If You're Buying This Month

Rates at 6.66% sting, no question. But look at what you're getting in exchange: sellers with 100+ days on market, softening asking prices per square foot, and a thinning pool of competing buyers. August through September is historically when Flathead Valley buyers get concessions that were unthinkable in June — price adjustments, rate buydowns, inspection repairs actually negotiated instead of waived.

The move is to be selective and decisive at the same time. On stale listings, the data is your leverage — days on market and the asking-to-sold gap justify a serious-but-under-ask offer. On well-priced fresh listings, don't get cute: the good ones still go, because every other serious buyer in the valley is watching the same shrinking pool of new inventory you are.

And on rates: marry the house, date the rate is a cliché, but run the actual math with your lender on what a future refinance would look like before you let 6.66% talk you out of a home that fits.

## The Bottom Line

The August turn isn't a downturn — it's a changing of the guard. Casual sellers and casual buyers leave the field. What's left is a smaller, more serious market where preparation and pricing discipline decide outcomes. That's not a market to fear. That's a market to plan for.

So here's the question worth sitting with this week: if you listed in the next 30 days, would you be entering the market priced like June — or priced like August?
