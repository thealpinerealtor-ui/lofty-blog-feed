Rates dropped again last week. Second week in a row.

If you're waiting on rates before you buy or sell in the Flathead Valley, that headline probably felt like the thing you've been waiting for. So let's do something most market updates skip: let's put an actual dollar figure on it.

A tenth of a point sounds like a lot. Priced out in monthly payment, it isn't — and set next to what local sellers are doing to their asking prices, the whole "wait for rates" strategy starts to look backwards.

It's Monday. Numbers first, then the read.

## The Numbers: Late August 2026

**Rates.** The Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed at 6.65% as of August 20, 2026 — down from 6.67% the week before, with the 15-year at 5.95%. That's two consecutive weekly declines (source: Freddie Mac PMMS, August 20, 2026).

Worth noting the part the headlines leave off: a year ago the 30-year averaged 6.58%. We are still *above* where we were last August. This isn't a rate rally. It's a drift.

**Kalispell.** For the period July 23 through August 23, 2026 (source: Montana Regional MLS data via IDEAL Real Estate's Kalispell market report, August 2026 period):

- New listings: 93, down 13.1% from the prior month
- Active inventory: 510 properties, down 1.2%
- Days on market: 113, up 8.7%
- Price changes: **157 listings adjusted their price, up 30.8%**
- Average list price on active inventory: $993,000, up 6.2%

**The wider valley.** For July 2026 across northwest Montana — Flathead, Lincoln, and Lake counties — the median sales price was $612,500, down 2.8% year over year. Closed sales were up 14.9%, average days on market was 104, inventory stood at 1,479 homes (up 11.5% year over year), and the average seller received 97.3% of asking price (source: Northwest Montana Association of REALTORS®, July 2026 market update).

Hold onto two of those: **157 price cuts** and **$612,500**.

## Now the Math Nobody Does For You

Take that $612,500 median. Put 20% down. You're financing $490,000.

At 6.65%, principal and interest runs about **$3,146 a month**.

Now run the rate move everyone's celebrating. Two weeks ago the survey read 6.69%. Today it's 6.65%. That four-basis-point drop is worth — brace yourself — **about $13 a month**.

Widen it out. A full tenth of a point, 6.75% down to 6.65%, is worth **$32.50 a month**. In purchasing power, that tenth buys you roughly **$5,000** more house at the same payment.

Five thousand dollars. On a $612,500 home. That's the rate story.

Now run the other number. A 3% price reduction on that same median home is $18,375 — and it drops the payment by **$118 a month**.

Same house. Same buyer. The price cut is worth **nine times** what the two-week rate drop is worth.

And there were 157 price cuts in Kalispell last month.

## What This Means If You're Buying

You have been told to wait for rates. The math says wait for *sellers* — and they're already moving.

One hundred fifty-seven price adjustments, up nearly 31% in a month, on inventory averaging 113 days on market. That is not a market where you need a Fed decision to improve your position. Those sellers are doing the arithmetic at their own kitchen tables right now.

Here's the trap in the wait-for-rates plan: rate relief is public and simultaneous. If the 30-year drops to 6.15% next spring, every sidelined buyer gets the same $164 a month you do — and they all show up the same weekend, bidding on the same 510 houses. Cheaper money that arrives with a crowd isn't cheaper. It's just a bigger line.

Negotiating leverage is private and temporary. The seller at 113 days who's cut twice is negotiating with *you*, not with the market. And the price you negotiate is permanent — baked into your basis, your equity, your loan balance for as long as you own the place. The rate is the part you can change later. Refinancing a price you overpaid isn't a thing.

## What This Means If You're Selling

If you've been holding your number waiting for rates to rescue your listing, look at what rates just did for your buyer: thirteen dollars a month.

Thirteen dollars does not un-stall a listing. Your buyer isn't sitting out over $13. They're sitting out over the gap between your asking price and the last three comparable closings on your side of town — and that gap is measured in tens of thousands, not basis points.

The valley-wide numbers say something useful here, though, and it isn't all bad news: closed sales were up 14.9% year over year in July, and sellers averaged 97.3% of asking. Homes are moving, and the ones priced right aren't getting gutted at the negotiating table. The 3% haircut is landing on the *listing* price, not the *right* price.

There's a tailwind coming, too. New listings are down 13% and will keep thinning through fall, so a correctly priced home in September faces the least competition it's seen since May. Just don't confuse "less competition" with "hold the number." Reposition once, deliberately, to what's actually closing near you, and let the thin inventory work.

## The Bottom Line

Rates fell twice in a row and it's worth about thirteen dollars a month on a median Flathead home. Local sellers cut prices 157 times in thirty days, and each one of those is worth roughly nine times more to the buyer than the rate move was.

The lever that's moving in this market is price, not rate. Buyers have negotiating room right now that disappears the moment cheaper money brings everyone back. Sellers have a shrinking-inventory window that only pays off if the number is honest on day one.

So here's the question worth sitting with this week: if you're buying, are you waiting on a rate cut worth $33 a month while passing on sellers who've already cut $18,000? And if you're selling, do you know what your street actually closed at in the last 60 days — or are you still priced to a spring number and hoping rates fix it?

Either way, the answer is specific to your address, not the valley average. That's a fifteen-minute conversation.
