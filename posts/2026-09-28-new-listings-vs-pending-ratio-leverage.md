# New Listings vs. Pending: The Ratio That Shows Who Has the Leverage

Most people watching the Flathead market check one number: how long is this house going to sit. Days on market is useful, but it's a lagging indicator — it tells you what already happened to a listing, not what's about to happen to the market underneath it.

There's a better number, and almost nobody outside the industry watches it: the ratio between new listings and pending sales. It moves before price does, and before days-on-market does, because it's measuring something more basic — is fresh supply outrunning demand, or is demand eating supply faster than it's arriving.

## The Current Numbers

As of today, Flathead County has 95 new listings currently on the market against 368 homes in pending status — under contract, working their way to closing (Redfin county-level data, live as of September 28, 2026).

Run that as a ratio and you get roughly one new listing for every 3.9 homes already locked into a pending contract. Put differently: for every fresh property landing on the market this week, close to four others are already off the table, just waiting to close.

That's a meaningfully lopsided ratio, and it's worth comparing against the backdrop most Flathead readers already know: countywide median days on market sat at 77 in the most recent monthly read, up from 62 a year ago (Movoto Kalispell market data, period through August 2026). Those two facts look like they're pulling in opposite directions — a slower market on one hand, a pending pipeline that dwarfs new supply on the other — and that tension is exactly the point. Days on market measures how long the *average* listing has been sitting, including the ones that are struggling. The new-listings-to-pending ratio measures the front door and the back door of the market at the same time, right now, and right now the back door — homes going under contract — is moving a lot more inventory than the front door is bringing in.

## What a Ratio This Lopsided Actually Telegraphs

When pending sales run this far ahead of new listings, it usually means one of two things is happening, and it pays to know which one you're standing in.

The first possibility: real demand is absorbing inventory faster than sellers are willing to list, which tightens the market for buyers even while headline days-on-market numbers look soft. A buyer waiting on the sidelines for "more to choose from" may be waiting on a shrinking pool, not a growing one — the well-priced, well-prepared listings are getting picked off into pending status well before the average 77-day clock runs out, and what's left sitting on the market disproportionately skews toward the properties that are overpriced or otherwise sticky. If that's the case, the buyers who move now, on the right house, are the ones who benefit. The buyers who wait for the market to "come to them" may find the ratio has already told them the good inventory left first.

The second possibility: a batch of pendings from earlier in the summer is finally working through financing and appraisal and hasn't closed yet, inflating the pending count relative to a genuinely slower new-listing flow this particular week. That's a timing artifact, not a trend — and it's exactly why this is a number to watch over a few weeks, not read off a single Tuesday.

Either way, the ratio is the earliest signal either side gets. Price adjusts last. Days-on-market adjusts second. The new-listings-to-pending ratio is the one that moves first, because it's counting behavior — sellers deciding to list, buyers deciding to commit — instead of counting outcomes.

## The Rate Backdrop

None of this happens in a vacuum. Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.03% for the week ending September 24, 2026, up from 6.95% the week before and well above the 6.30% average from the same week a year ago. A rate that's climbed roughly three-quarters of a point year-over-year is part of why sellers are more cautious about listing right now — trading a known rate for an unknown one is a real cost — which helps explain the thin new-listings side of this week's ratio even as pending activity holds up.

## What To Do With This This Week

If you're a seller watching your neighbor's listing sit at day 40 or 50 and wondering whether the market has stalled: the ratio says otherwise. Demand hasn't disappeared — it's just being more selective about which listings it's absorbing into pending status, and the ones getting picked are the ones priced and presented correctly the first time.

If you're a buyer waiting for a bigger pool to shop from: this week's numbers suggest the opposite is more likely. The properties disappearing into "pending" are outnumbering the ones showing up "new" by nearly four to one. Waiting for more selection, in this particular ratio environment, may just mean watching the same properties you'd have wanted go under contract without you.

Neither of those is a reason to rush a decision you're not ready to make. It's a reason to know which side of the ratio you're actually standing on before you decide to wait.
