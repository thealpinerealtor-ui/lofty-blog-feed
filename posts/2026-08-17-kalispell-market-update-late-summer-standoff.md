If you've been watching the Kalispell and Whitefish market this month and thinking "something doesn't add up," you're reading it right.
Inventory is shrinking. Homes are sitting longer. And asking prices are going *up* anyway.
Those three things aren't supposed to happen at the same time. When they do, it means the market has settled into a standoff — sellers holding their number, buyers holding their ground, and both sides waiting to see who blinks first as summer winds down.
It's Monday. Numbers first, then the read.
## The Numbers: Mid-August 2026
Here's the most recent data for Kalispell and the surrounding market, covering July 16 through August 16, 2026 (source: Montana Regional MLS data via IDEAL Real Estate's Kalispell market report, mid-August 2026 period):
New listings came in at 93 — down another 12.3% from the prior period. That follows the 36% drop we saw at the start of the month. Fresh inventory isn't just slowing; it's drying up on schedule, the way it does every year once the back-to-school calendar takes over.
Active inventory sits at 511 properties, down about 1% month over month. The summer stack has crested and is slowly thinning.
Days on market for active listings stretched to 110, up 6% from the prior period. The homes still on the market are the ones that have been sitting — and they're sitting longer.
Price changes: 155 listings cut or adjusted their price this period, up 18.3%. Nearly one in three active listings has moved its number. Remember that stat — it matters more than any other one here.
And yet: the average list price on active inventory rose to $950,000 (up 1.6%), and average asking price per square foot jumped to $408, up 15% from the prior period.
On rates: the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed at 6.67% as of August 13, 2026, down slightly from 6.69% the week before, with the 15-year at 5.96% (source: Freddie Mac PMMS, August 13, 2026). Rates have been parked in the mid-6s for weeks — stable, not helpful.
## Wait — Prices Are Up 15%?
No. And this is where most people misread the data.
That $408-per-square-foot number describes what's *asking*, not what's *selling*. When entry-level and mid-range homes go under contract and don't get replaced by new listings — which is exactly what a 12% drop in new inventory does in August — the leftover pool skews toward the expensive end. Lakefront, acreage, the big log home on the hill. The average rises because the mix changed, not because your house gained 15% in thirty days.
The honest read is the opposite of what the headline number suggests: 155 price reductions in one month is the market telling sellers, one listing at a time, that the asking prices are ahead of the buyers. That's the real signal. The rest is math doing what math does when the cheap homes leave the pool.
This is why I keep saying: don't price your home off a portal average. Price it off what actually closed in your neighborhood in the last 60 days.
## What This Means If You're Selling
If your home has been sitting past that 110-day average, you're not "waiting for the right buyer." You're in the price-reduction cohort — you just haven't made it official yet. The listings that sell in this market are the ones priced at the market on day one, while the 30% doing serial price cuts follow the market down and end up netting less than if they'd started right.
Here's the part working in your favor: your competition is evaporating. New listings are down double digits and will keep falling through fall. A well-priced, well-presented home in September faces less competition than at any point since May. If you missed the summer window, don't panic-list at a stale number — reposition once, correctly, and let the thin inventory do its work.
## What This Means If You're Buying
A standoff favors the patient side — and right now, that's you.
One hundred fifty-five price reductions is 155 sellers who've already admitted their number was wrong. Homes at 110+ days on market have sellers doing math at the kitchen table every night. That's where negotiation room lives: seller credits, rate buydowns, inspection concessions that didn't exist in June.
And a rate note worth doing the arithmetic on: 6.67% isn't the rate you'll have forever — it's the rate you'll have until the first refinance window opens. The price you negotiate in a standoff, though, is permanent. Buyers who wait for rates to drop will re-enter the market alongside everyone else who had the same idea, and the leverage you have this fall goes with them.
## The Bottom Line
The mid-August market isn't hot and it isn't broken. It's a standoff — thinning inventory holding prices up on paper, longer market times and a wave of price cuts pulling them down in practice. Sellers who price to the closed data (not the asking averages) still sell. Buyers willing to engage with a 100-day listing instead of waiting for a unicorn are getting terms nobody got in spring.
Both of those moves require knowing your specific street, not the valley-wide average. That's a fifteen-minute conversation.
So here's the question worth sitting with this week: if you're selling, do you actually know what closed near you in the last 60 days — or are you anchored to a number from June? And if you're buying, which of those 110-day listings would you look at differently if you knew the seller had already cut twice?
