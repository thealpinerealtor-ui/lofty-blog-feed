---
title: "Ask Versus Close: What Flathead Sellers Are Actually Getting"
description: Every seller watches the list price. Almost none watch the gap between what a house asks and what it actually closes at — and right now in the Flathead, that gap is where the real story is.
---
Every seller in the Flathead Valley can quote their neighbor's list price from memory. Almost nobody can tell you what that house actually closed at, or how many times the number changed between the sign going up and the sale going final.

That's backwards. The list price is a guess. The close price is the truth. And the spread between the two — plus how many times a seller had to move the ask to get there — is the single clearest read on where this market actually sits right now.

Numbers first, then what to do with them.

## The Numbers: Mid-August Through Mid-September

For Kalispell, the period August 13 through September 13, 2026 (source: Montana Regional MLS data via IDEAL Real Estate's Kalispell market report, that period):

- Active listings: 503, up 2.03% from the prior month
- New listings: 104
- Average asking price on active listings: $953,000 — down 10.24% from the prior month
- Average original list price on that same pool: roughly $968,000
- Price reductions: 141 listings, up sharply and now representing better than one in four active listings
- Average days on market: 115.84 days, up 3.43%

Separately, Montana Regional MLS figures reported through Movoto put Kalispell's median closed sale price at $699,000 for August 2026, with median days on market at 77 — up from 62 days a year ago.

On rates: Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed at 6.76% as of September 10, 2026, up slightly from 6.71% the week before.

A clean, published original-list-to-final-close ratio isn't available for this market this month — the MLS feeds that would produce it don't break it out publicly at the county level. But the two things that stand in for it, the reduction rate and the days-on-market trend, are telling the same story from two different directions, and it's worth reading them together instead of picking whichever one is more comfortable.

## What the Reduction Rate Is Actually Telling You

More than one in four active Kalispell listings has already had at least one price cut. That's not a handful of overpriced outliers — that's a market-wide signal. When a quarter of the inventory needs a correction to keep moving, the problem isn't a few sellers who got greedy. It's that the starting assumption a lot of listings were priced against — where the market was a few months ago — has already moved on by the time the sign goes in the yard.

The average asking price on active listings has fallen roughly 10% in a single month, from an original average list closer to $968,000 down to $953,000 now showing. Some of that is new, more realistically priced inventory coming on. A meaningful piece of it is existing listings getting corrected because the first number didn't work.

Either way, the direction is the same: sellers are giving ground after listing, not before. That's the expensive way to find the right price.

## Days on Market Is the Other Half of the Story

Average days on market sitting at 115.84 days — and separately, median days on market up to 77 from 62 a year ago — both point the same direction. Homes are taking longer to move than they were twelve months ago, and every extra week a listing sits is a week where a buyer sees the "days on market" counter climbing on the listing page and starts wondering what's wrong with the house rather than what's wrong with the price.

That's the mechanism worth understanding: a stale listing doesn't just wait for the right buyer. It actively signals something to every buyer who scrolls past it. By the time a seller finally cuts the price to where it should have started, they're often negotiating against their own listing history, not just the market.

## What This Means If You're Pricing Right Now

If you're getting ready to list, the question isn't "what did the house down the street ask for." It's "what did the house down the street actually close at, and how many cuts did it take to get there." That number exists — your agent can pull it from sold comps — and it's a far better anchor than any asking price still sitting active on the market.

If you're already listed and haven't had a single showing in three weeks, the reduction-rate math above should tell you something: more than a quarter of your competition has already adjusted. Waiting to be the last one to move is not a strategy, it's a cost, measured in the extra days-on-market number that follows the listing around.

The sellers doing well in this market right now aren't the ones who priced highest. They're the ones who priced against where the market actually is today, took the showings that came in the first two weeks seriously as real feedback, and made one clean adjustment early instead of three small ones late.

If you're wondering whether your number is priced against last month's market or this one, that's worth a direct conversation before the listing goes live — not after it's been sitting for ten weeks.
