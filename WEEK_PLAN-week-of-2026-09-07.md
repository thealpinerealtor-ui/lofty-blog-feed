## Monday, September 7 — Local market update
**Working title:** The 90-Day Listings: What Aging Inventory Is Telling Flathead Sellers
**Angle:** Pull the current Flathead County / Montana Regional MLS picture, then split it the way
sellers never do: not "how is the market," but "how are homes that have been listed 90+ days
doing versus homes listed in the last 30." The spread between those two groups says more about
pricing than any median does. Cody May / Leo Pareja treatment — the number first, then the
uncomfortable read: when a whole valley's inventory is sitting, it's the market; when the house
next door sold in three weeks and yours didn't, it isn't.

## Tuesday, September 8 — Divorce home sales
**Working title:** Two Attorneys, One Listing Agent: How Divorcing Couples Choose Who Sells the House
**Angle:** Before anything gets listed, both parties have to agree on who lists it — and that
decision gets made badly more often than any other in this process. Covers why a single neutral
listing agent is usually cleaner than each side bringing their own, what "neutral" actually
means in practice (identical information to both parties, no private side conversations, both
signatures on price decisions, written communication as the default), the questions each spouse
should ask before agreeing to an agent, what to do when one party insists on their own agent or
a family friend, and how the listing agreement itself gets structured when there are two sellers
who aren't speaking. Empathetic, zero judgment, no side-taking. "Your attorney drives what gets
filed and what the decree requires — this is the process side only."

## Wednesday, September 9 — Buyer guides
**Working title:** New Construction or Resale in Kalispell: What You're Actually Choosing
**Angle:** Not a pros-and-cons list — a decision framework. What the builder's contract does
that a resale contract doesn't (deposit structure, allowance lines, change orders, completion
dates that move, warranty terms, and who the builder's representative actually works for), what
inspections still matter on a brand-new house, how lot premiums and finished-out costs land on
the final number, what a resale gives you that a spec home can't (mature trees, known
neighbors' road agreements, an actual utility history, a septic with a record), and the timing
math when you're selling something else to buy. Kalispell-specific: the new subdivisions versus
the established in-town stock.

## Thursday, September 10 — Estate & probate sales
**Working title:** Before You Can Sell: What Montana Probate Actually Requires First
**Angle:** Families call about listing an inherited home well before anyone has authority to
sell it, and nobody has explained the gap. Plain-English walk through the sequence: what
happens between the death and the point where a personal representative can legally sign a
listing and a deed, what letters testamentary / letters of administration are and why title
companies ask for them, the difference between informal and formal probate in Montana at a
high level, roughly how long each stage runs, what can be done in the meantime (securing the
house, insurance on a vacant property, utilities, a broker's opinion of value for the
inventory), and what a small-estate situation or a property already in a trust changes.
Compassionate and practical, never rushing the family. Explicit disclaimer: this is a general
overview, not legal advice — confirm the estate's authority and timeline with the attorney
handling it.

## Friday, September 11 — Community & lifestyle
**Working title:** Twenty Minutes From the Mountain: What Whitefish Winter Access Really Buys
**Angle:** Early September is when the valley starts thinking about ski season, and buyers
relocating here consistently misjudge what "close to the mountain" is worth. An amenities-first
look at winter access: the drive up Big Mountain Road and what it's like in weather, the
difference between in-town Whitefish, the Highway 93 corridor, and Columbia Falls or Kalispell
on a January morning, what the shuttle and season-pass calendar look like, parking and lift
realities on a holiday weekend, and the trade between proximity and everything else a house
has to be for the other eight months. Places, roads, and amenities only — no characterization
of who lives where. Measured tone; this publishes the week of September 11.

## Saturday, September 12 — Seller guides
**Working title:** The First Showing Happens on a Phone Screen
**Angle:** Sellers prepare the house and then let the listing media go out unmanaged — and the
photos are where nine out of ten buyers decide whether the house exists. What a listing
actually needs (shot count and sequence, the hero image, wide-angle honesty versus distortion,
twilight and drone shots and when they help versus when they oversell), why the shoot day
matters more than anything staged the week before, what fall light and green grass are worth
if the house may still be listed in January, floor plans and why they cut wasted showings,
and how to tell a good listing photographer's work from a bad one before you hire. Distinct
from the staging piece — this is the media and marketing side, not the furniture.

## Sunday, September 13 — Brand / authority
**Working title:** The Plan You Hope You Never Open
**Angle:** Every operation gets a primary plan and a contingency, and the contingency is
written before anyone steps off — not improvised at the moment it's needed. Applied to a
transaction: what happens if the appraisal comes in short, if financing falls apart ten days
out, if the inspection turns up something structural, if the buyer's own sale collapses. Each
of those has a pre-decided response, discussed with the client while everything is calm, so the
call that delivers bad news also delivers the next three steps. The point isn't pessimism —
it's that panic is a planning failure, and clients feel the difference. Anonymized, no agency
named, no war stories, no hype.
