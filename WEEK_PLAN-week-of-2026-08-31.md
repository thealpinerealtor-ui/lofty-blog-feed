**Working title:** The Fall Window: What September Actually Does to Flathead Prices
**Angle:** Every seller in the valley has been told "wait for spring." This one looks at what
the September–November stretch has historically done to Flathead County inventory, days on
market, and closed price versus list — and who the fall buyer actually is (relocating,
job-timed, cash, unhurried, and far more likely to still be looking in November than the

## Tuesday, September 1 — Divorce home sales
**Working title:** When One Spouse Won't Cooperate with the Sale
**Angle:** One of the hardest calls in this niche: the decision to sell is made, and one party
stops responding — won't sign the listing, won't allow showings, won't leave for an open house.
What the process actually looks like when cooperation breaks down: why the agent has to stay a
neutral third party and communicate to both sides identically, how showing and access protocols
get written into the listing agreement up front, what a court can order regarding sale and
access in Montana, and why documenting every request in writing protects the person acting in
good faith. Empathetic, zero judgment, no side-taking, no legal advice — "your attorney drives
what gets filed."

## Wednesday, September 2 — Buyer guides
**Working title:** Buying Before the Snow: What to Check on a Flathead Home in September
**Angle:** A house shown in September tells you almost nothing about how it lives in February.
What to inspect while the weather still lets you: roof age, pitch and evidence of ice damming,
attic insulation and ventilation, the heat source and its backup (propane tank ownership and
fill history, wood stove condition and clearances, whether the heat pump is rated for real
cold), pipe runs in unheated crawlspaces, gutter and drainage grading, and the one nobody
asks about — who plows the road, under what agreement, and what it costs. Checklist-driven and
distinct from the well-and-septic and raw-land pieces by staying on the structure and access side.

## Thursday, September 3 — Estate & probate sales
**Working title:** Clearing Out the House: The Part Nobody Plans For
**Angle:** The legal side of an estate gets a checklist. The garage doesn't. This is the piece
about the personal property — why families stall here longer than anywhere else, a workable
sequence for sorting (documents and valuables first, then keep/distribute/sell/donate/haul),
how estate-sale companies and consignment actually price and charge, what to do when heirs live
out of state and can't all be in the room, and how to sequence the clear-out against the listing
so the house isn't sitting empty for four months or photographed full. Compassionate, practical,
never rushing the family; "confirm anything about the estate's authority with your attorney."

## Friday, September 4 — Community & lifestyle
**Working title:** Columbia Falls and the Canyon: The Glacier Side of the Valley
**Angle:** The stretch from Columbia Falls up the Middle Fork toward West Glacier is the part of
the Flathead people drive through on the way to the park and rarely look at as a place to live.
An amenities-first spotlight: the closest paved access to Glacier's west entrance, the Middle
Fork and North Fork corridors, Big Mountain visible one way and the Bob Marshall the other,
what's in town year-round versus what's seasonal, commute realities to Kalispell and Whitefish,
and how the housing stock differs from the Whitefish core. Places and amenities only — no
characterizations of who lives where.

## Saturday, September 5 — Seller guides
**Working title:** The Pre-Listing Inspection: Finding It Before the Buyer Does
**Angle:** Most sellers first hear about their roof from a buyer's inspector, at the worst
possible moment — under contract, with leverage on the other side of the table. This covers
what a pre-listing inspection costs against what a mid-transaction repair request typically
costs, how to triage findings into fix / price-in / disclose-and-move-on, why fixing everything
is usually the wrong call, what Montana disclosure obligations mean once you know something
(with "confirm with your broker and attorney"), and how a clean report used correctly becomes a
pricing argument instead of a liability. Complements last Saturday's pricing piece from the
condition side.

## Sunday, September 6 — Brand / authority
**Working title:** Slow Is Smooth, Smooth Is Fast
**Angle:** The line gets repeated until it sounds like a poster, but it's a real operating
principle: the fastest outcome almost never comes from the fastest movement. It comes from
rehearsed sequence — knowing the next three steps before the current one finishes, so nothing
has to be redone. Applied to a transaction: the deadline calendar built the day the contract is
signed, the lender call made before the appraisal is ordered, the repair addendum drafted before
the inspection report lands. Clients experience it as a deal that never felt rushed and still
closed on time. Anonymized, no agency named, no war stories, no hype.
